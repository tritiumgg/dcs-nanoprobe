This document states the method and the limits, written before the long run, the
rehearsal of the tool, and the live runs with their verdict. Three parts of the method
changed after the run, and "Live runs" says how: the rising rule for a series without
a slope limit, when a group may be given another route, and the guard that compares
the churn a run achieved with its plan.

## What a run records

One record a minute, on one clock: record `k` is due `k` minutes after the churn
begins and carries the time it was due and how late it was taken.

| Series | Source | Under Wine |
|---|---|---|
| Lua heap floors, `server` and `hook` state | A forced full collect through the eval channel every 15 minutes, and the heap right after it | The agent's shim runs in `server`; its loader and the eval hook run in `hook` |
| DCS resident memory, threads | `/proc/<pid>/status` of the DCS process | The Wine process that runs `DCS_server.exe` |
| Container working set | The cgroup's memory less its inactive file cache, and the cgroup's limit | What the limit that kills DCS counts |
| Handles | Open file descriptors of DCS, of the Wine server and of the sidecar (`/proc/<pid>/fd`), and the Wine server's resident memory | A Windows handle is an object in the Wine server, and Linux has no count of them. Descriptors and the Wine server's memory are the proxy. The Windows handle count is not measured (UNVERIFIED that the proxy tracks it) |
| Sidecar resident memory, CPU, threads, descriptors | `/proc` of the sidecar's process. Its container shares the game container's process namespace for the run, because its image has no shell | |
| Object IDs | The largest runtime object ID among the run's units (`id_`), against the units spawned | `id_` steps by 256 per spawned unit in the pilot and the control (LIVE, "Live runs") |
| The agent's own sizes | The last `sampling` report of the minute: `rows`, `births`, `pending_destroy`, `pending_deaths`, `loadouts_waiting` | The sampler's set of destroyed objects has no report field; it grows by one entry per destroyed object by design (docs/results/2026-11-14-s11-budget-and-baseline.md, open items) |
| Slowest steps | The largest `enum_step_max_us` and `birth_step_max_us` of the minute's reports | |
| Agent overhead per window | The minute's mean `share` and the share of frames over 250 µs, and the same over the whole run | |
| Store | File sizes of `store.sqlite`, its WAL, `events.sqlite` and its WAL every minute; journal rows by kind, entity rows and the export's bytes through `ExportCampaign` every 30 minutes; event rows from the subscription; the sidecar's commit and checkpoint times by quarter of the run, from its log | |
| DCS health | Frame rate over a 10 s window, its 5th percentile, model time against real time, DCS's CPU share, players connected | The soak counts the agent's frames (`lua/sampling/fps.lua`); the control counts the calls of a waiting eval snippet, one a frame, so it loads no agent |

## Churn and why

The campaign holds 1,000 groups of 4 M-113 on a land grid 300 m apart. 250 groups
(1,000 units) are in DCS and 750 are virtual. Every 30 s one step:

| Per step | Count | Per hour |
|---|---|---|
| Groups dematerialized, and as many materialized | 4 | 480 each |
| Groups removed (`RemoveEntity`) and created again (`PutGroup`) | 1 | 120 |
| Route orders (`SetOrder`): 4 laps of a 200 m square in the group's own cell at 5 m/s, to groups not driving one; a route takes 640 s, after which its group can be routed or killed again | 3 | 360 |
| Damage effects (`DoEffect`) | 2 | 240 |
| Area queries (`QueryArea`), world lists (`ListWorld`) | 1 each | 120 each |
| A group not driving a route killed every fourth step, with an explosion called on each of its units that still exists, through the scripting engine (the API's `Explosion` effect is not built); its dead group is removed and created again | 1/4 | 30 groups, 120 unit deaths |

A world watch and an event subscription stay open for the whole run, and the sidecar
takes a scheduled backup every 3 hours.

Sizing:

- **1,000 units in DCS, about a fifth driving.** DCS held 112 fps at 1,200 objects with
  a fifth routed through 90 minutes of churn, and 50 to 63 fps with stalls (5th
  percentile near 0.4 fps) at 2,000 (LIVE,
  docs/results/2026-11-14-s11-budget-and-baseline.md). 1,000 leaves the simulation core
  room for spawn bursts and a visitor.
- **Turnover in 24 minutes.** 5.25 groups leave and enter DCS per step, so the 250 live
  groups turn over in 48 steps, S11's rate: about 2,500 units spawned and destroyed an
  hour. A leak of 1 KiB per spawned unit is 2.4 MiB an hour.
- **Routes inside a cell.** A route takes 11 minutes, 3 are given per step, so about 60
  groups drive at a time, and no route leaves its group's 300 m cell.
- **120 wrecks an hour.** A killed unit leaves a wreck. 8 hours leave 960. S11's
  90-minute soak held 112 fps (LIVE, same document) while it killed about 2,250 units:
  25 units a step, 180 steps, explosions on every other step (derived from that soak's
  churn rate, not counted).
- **Memory guard.** A run ends itself when the container's working set passes 90 % of
  its limit, and keeps its records.

## The control and what differs

The control runs with no agent loaded: the agent's loader is taken out of the profile
before DCS starts and put back after the run. The eval hook stays, and
`lua/scenarios/soak.lua` drives DCS with plain scripting calls at the same places, in
the same order and numbers: groups destroyed and spawned for the handovers and the
replacements, routes set through the group's controller, and explosions on one group
every fourth step.

Differences from the soak:

- No sidecar and no client, so no store, no captures before a destroy, no sampling
  and no API queries.
- No damage effects.
- A handed-over group is a new DCS group with a new name each time. In the soak the
  same entity returns under its own name.
- The kill switch is not used to keep the agent out: the eval hook honors it too.

## Verdict rules

Each series is fitted by least squares after a warm-up: a quarter of the run, and at
most an hour. Dense series are fitted over the medians of 10-minute blocks, because
minute samples of memory are correlated and a fit over them understates its noise.
Sparse series (heap floors, journal counts) are fitted over their points. The noise of
a slope is twice its standard error.

- **Flat**: slope plus noise is at most the limit.
- **Growing**: slope minus noise is over the limit.
- **Unresolved**: neither.
- **Settled**: growing over the whole span, with the second half flat. A settled
  series passes: its growth ended inside the run, so it is neither unresolved nor
  rising at the end. The verdict lists it, and the results say what filled (a WAL
  climbing to its mark, a bounded list filling).
- **Rising**: the second half's slope exceeds the first half's by more than their
  noise and by more than 10 % of the first half's, and stays over the limit. A series
  without a slope limit is rising only when the second half's slope, less its noise,
  is above zero and above the first half's slope plus its noise (amended after the
  run, "Live runs"); with a control it must also be above the control's slope plus
  the control's noise. For a
  series the control also has, the second half's slope must be over the control's
  slope plus the limit, and without a control the series is not called rising: the
  control's slope is DCS's own. A count
  that follows the churn has almost no noise, and under 10 % the 24-hour projection
  moves by under 10 %.
- **Difference from the control**: for a series the control also has, the soak is
  fitted over the control's own window of uptime, and the platform's slope is the
  soak's less the control's, with the two noises combined. That difference is what the
  limit judges. Without a control such a series has no verdict, whatever it reads
  alone.
- **After the control's window**: the control ends after 2 hours and the soak goes on.
  DCS's own slope is taken to stay at the control's, and the soak's slope over the
  rest of the run, less the control's, is judged against the same limit. A series over
  it there is growing, whatever the first 2 hours showed.
- **The control itself**: a folder is a control when it is a control run of the same
  target during which the agent's log did not grow. A control shorter than 2 hours, or
  one that did not run to its end, is used and the verdict is not conclusive. A control
  run during which the agent's log grew is invalid.
- **Projection to 24 hours**: the fitted value at the end plus the whole-span slope for
  the hours left, and the same with the noise added. A series with a cap fails when
  the upper projection passes it.

A DCS or sidecar restart ends a segment. The longest segment is fitted, with time
counted from its start.

The run passes when no series is growing, no projection passes its cap and the owner's
targets hold. It is extended, not passed, when a series is unresolved or rising, has no
data or lacks its control, when the fitted segment of a campaign soak is shorter than
8 hours or the run did not go to its end, and when a kind of churn fell under 80 % of
its planned rate for 90 minutes (`churn_short`, added after the run). A run the memory
guard ended fails.

## Stated limits

| Series | Limit | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Heap floors, `server` and `hook` | Platform slope at most 1 MiB an hour | DCS's own floor grew about 7.2 and 7.8 MB an hour with and without the agent in S11's paired runs (LIVE). The 0.6 MB between them is what a pair of runs cannot tell apart, so 1 MiB an hour is the smallest platform slope a pair can show. It is 24 MiB in a day |
| DCS resident memory | Platform slope at most 2 % of the mean an hour | S11's paired runs differed by up to 1.6 % of the mean an hour in either direction with no effect of the agent (LIVE: 9.9 against 11.5, 12.2 against 10.9). A tighter limit is inside that spread |
| Container working set | Projected to 24 hours, at most 80 % of the container's limit | With no swap the limit kills DCS. This judges the 24-hour scale target whoever causes the growth |
| Descriptors and threads of DCS, the Wine server and the sidecar | Slope at most 1 an hour | Anything faster is a leak; 1 an hour reaches no limit in a day |
| Wine server resident memory | Platform slope at most 1 MiB an hour | Where a leaked handle's object stays |
| Sidecar resident memory | Slope at most 16 MiB an hour; projected to 24 hours, at most 1 GiB | The idempotency index keeps every changing call for 24 hours by design. 16 MiB an hour allows it, and a leak of 100 bytes per driver step (500 a second) is 172 MiB an hour |
| Object IDs per spawned unit | At most 281.6 (256 plus 10 %) | DCS steps object IDs by 256 per object (LIVE, S11) |
| Agent rows | Slope at most 1 % of the mean an hour | The churn holds the population constant |
| Agent pending sets (`pending_destroy`, `pending_deaths`, `loadouts_waiting`, `births`) | Slope at most 1 entry an hour | Each drains in steady state |
| Slowest enumeration step, slowest birth step | Not rising at the end; projected to 24 hours, at most 2,000 µs | S11 saw the enumeration's slowest step (`enum_step_max_us`) grow from 484 to 1,138 µs in 90 minutes and flatten. 2 ms is what the platform allows itself for commanded work in a frame |
| Agent share per window | Projected to 24 hours, at most 0.3 % | The owner's target for the mean |
| Frames over 250 µs per window | Projected to 24 hours, at most 1 % | The owner's target of a 99th percentile of at most 250 µs |
| Store and event history files | Not rising; projected to 24 hours, each at most 5 % of the free space of the store's file system at the start | Both grow by design until their retention deletes. Their growth per hour is the reported figure |
| WAL files | Slope at most 1 MiB an hour | SQLite checkpoints a WAL at 1,000 pages and reuses the file, so it climbs to its high-water mark, about 4 MiB, and holds it. A run shorter than the climb reads it as growing |
| Journal rows, event rows | Not rising | Reported per hour |
| Groups in the campaign | Slope at most 1 an hour | The churn removes and creates the same number |
| Entity rows in the store | Not rising | The store keeps a removed entity for good (spec/world.md, "Removal"), so the rows grow with every removal by design. Reported per hour |
| Sidecar CPU | Not rising; projected to 24 hours, at most 50 % of a core | The driver is one thread whose step walks every group the store holds, removed ones included |
| Owner targets, whole run | Mean share at most 0.3 %; at most 1 % of frames over 250 µs | GOAL criterion 1 |

## What a long run meets

| Event | What the tool does |
|---|---|
| A player joins | The record carries the player count and an event is logged. The run goes on |
| DCS stalls | A reading or a churn step that fails is logged with its reason and left out of the record. A record that cannot be taken in its minute is skipped. The run goes on |
| DCS or the sidecar restarts | The processes' PID and start time change. The segment ends. The tool waits for DCS, loads the campaign's mission when no player is connected, starts the sidecar from its store and continues in a new segment |
| The starting session ends | `--detach` runs the tool in a process group of its own with its output in a log. `soak status` lists the runs, and `soak stop <folder>` ends one at its next step with its verdict and cleanup |

## Rehearsal

The whole tool against `nanoprobe-fake-sidecar`: a record every 5 s, a churn step
every 3 s, 60 groups of 2 units with 20 in DCS. Host: macOS on Apple silicon, debug
builds, 2026-11-18, the tree of the commit that adds this document. The fake has no DCS
process, no Lua and no agent, so those series read "no data" and the live pilot proves
them. The sidecar's CPU also reads "no data": it is read from `/proc`, which the macOS
host lacks. The fake has no scripting engine either, so the rehearsal kills nothing. The
control's churn is rehearsed apart: `lua/tests/soak.lua` runs `lua/scenarios/soak.lua`
on the mock under stock Lua 5.1.

Four runs. The tool's own verdict on each is FAIL, as it must be: at 1,200 steps an
hour, ten times the live rate, the fake process grows faster than the live limit for
the sidecar allows, with and without a planted leak. The rehearsal's own pass rule is
below each run.

| Planted leak of 256 KiB a second, 6 minutes | `soak-rehearse-20261118T060248Z` | 72 | 1,003,000 ± 12,200 | FAIL (sidecar memory, event history WAL) | PASS |

is 2.8 % under the planted rate. The rehearsal passes when the excess is within 10 % of

process itself grows. The unit tests of `series.rs` and

**The baseline's own growth** is 107,000 KiB an hour, about 89 KiB a churn step. It is

### Fixes to the tool

The live runs found two faults in the control that the rehearsal could not show,
because the fake sidecar has no scripting engine and its eval channel no reply limit.
The review of the results found a third, in the campaign's churn, and the work after it
added a guard and two changes to `soak judge`. All are in
`crates/xtask/src/persistence/campaign_soak.rs`:

- **The land check in chunks.** The control asked the eval channel which of 2,304 grid
  points are on land in one reply. A reply holds at most 2,000 entries of an array
  (spec/eval.md), so the control stopped at setup with 2,001 answers. It now asks in
  chunks of 2,000, as the campaign's `virtualization::places` does, and takes the same
  places.
- **The control's frame rate without the agent.** `lua/sampling/fps.lua` counts the
  agent's frames, so it opens the agent library. In the control it loaded the agent
  DLL at each reading (an "agent configured" line in the agent's log) and timed out
  after 130 s, which made the control invalid. The control now counts frames with its
  own wait snippet, `lua/sampling/fps-control.lua`: the eval hook polls a waiting
  snippet once a frame (`lua/dev/eval-hook.lua`, `frame`), so each call is one frame.
  `lua/tests/fps_control.lua` runs it on the mock. Checked once live through `cargo
  xtask eval <target> hook` with a 10 s window: 111.07 fps by the snippet against the
  hook's own count of the request, 1,169 frames in 10.512 s, 111.2 fps (LIVE; the
  reply was read on the console and not kept in a bundle).
- **Routes that end.** A group given a route was marked routed for good, and both the
  route orders and the kill target take only groups not routed. Once every group in DCS
  had driven a route, no group could be killed, and only groups created again were
  given one. A route now ends after its laps, which take 640 s; the group counts as
  driving for the 23 steps of 30 s that cover them, and its group
  can then be routed or killed again. The test
  `a_route_ends_after_its_laps_so_its_group_can_be_routed_or_killed_again` checks the
  end of a route and that 8 hours of steps never run out of groups to route. The soak
  ran before this fix ("Soak").
- **A guard on the churn.** Each record carries the churn's totals by kind. The
  verdict counts each kind over consecutive half hours against the plan's rate
  (`planned_churn`: steps, handovers each way, removes and puts, routes, effects,
  queries, kills; the control's steps and routes), and a kind under 80 % of it for
  three half hours in a row, or one the plan has and no record counts, takes the
  verdict away: EXTEND, with the stretches listed in `churn_short` and in
  `verdict.md`. It extends rather than fails because the records show nothing wrong
  with the platform, only part of the load missing. Three half hours, because a stall
  or a DCS restart of up to 30 minutes touches at most two. A run under 90 minutes is
  not checked. The test `a_churn_that_falls_short_of_its_plan_takes_the_verdict_away`
  degrades the kills and routes mid-run as this soak did, and checks that a 30-minute
  stall and a steady run pass. On this soak's records it lists the routes from
  12,660 s (136 an hour against 360) and the kills from 10,860 s (2 an hour against 30).
- **`soak judge` keeps the run's provenance.** A second verdict rewrote `soak.json`
  with the build and times of the moment it was judged again. It now keeps the run's
  and records when it was judged again (`judged_again_at`). With `--from <s>` and
  `--to <s>` it judges a window of the records on the run's clock into
  `verdict-window-<from>-<to>.*`, leaving the run's verdict alone; a window that starts
  after the run's start is judged without the control, whose fit is matched to the
  first hours of uptime.

`soak control`, 2 hours, 06:10 to 08:14 EST, bundle
`soak-control-<target>-20261118T111028Z`. Verdict CONTROL: completed, 120 records,

## Journal retention

`[store] journal_retention_days` (spec/config.md; spec/persistence.md, "Journal
retention"), by the ruling on S21's design question:

- Whole days, default 0: the journal is kept for the life of the campaign. The default
  is one constant (`JOURNAL_RETENTION_DEFAULT`, `crates/api/src/config.rs`).
- Above 0, the active campaign's entries older than the retention are deleted at
  activation, at the sidecar's start, and once an hour. The delete takes the oldest
  entries in sequence order up to the first one inside the retention, and never the
  newest.
- It runs in batches of at most 1,000 entries, one per driver step, with the step's
  commit between two batches. One batch of 1,000 took about 0.7 ms in the hub test
  `a_journal_backlog_drains_one_batch_a_step_and_then_stops` (debug build, macOS on
  Apple silicon, 2026-11-18, the tree of the commit that adds this document).
- A suspended campaign's journal is pruned at its next activation. A finished
  campaign's is never deleted.

Host tests cover each rule: `crates/sidecar/src/store/disk.rs` (the store),
`crates/sidecar/src/export.rs` (the export's first line), `crates/api/src/hub.rs`
(the driver's batches, a suspended campaign, a finished campaign) and `crates/api/src/sidecar_toml.rs` (the
setting). `spec/transport.golden` and `spec/persistence.golden` are unchanged: the rule
adds no table, no code and no encoding.
