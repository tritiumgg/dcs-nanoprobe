## Cause

A group is `live` when its spawn's result is `APPLIED`. A unit of it is bound when the
sidecar has read its `OBJECT` record. On main the births tier wrote that record, after
it resolved the unit's birth event. Two rules of the platform set the time between the
two. DCS sets none of it: the birth event arrives in the spawn's frame (LIVE,
docs/results/2026-11-17-s20-virtualization.md), so the object exists then.

**The order.** The shim kept the births in a list and resolved the newest first. While
births arrive faster than they resolve, a backlog builds, and the first units of a spawn
stream wait until every later birth has resolved. `lua/tests/births.lua` adds 80 units
in two waves and reads the order of their `OBJECT` records: with the shim of main
`ac84a53` in place of this step's (the same test and agent, on the development machine,
2026-11-18) the first record was the 80th unit born, and the test failed there.

**The rate.** Births and the enumeration have half the 0.3 % budget (spec/sampling.md,
"Tiers"), and each pump pays its cross-state call from it. On the server, births resolved
at 26 to 34 a second while births waited (LIVE, the `sampling` reports of the branch run
of docs/results/2026-11-17-s19-spawn-state.md, agent `ff9c364bba4372c6`, bundle
`sampling-<target>-20261117T195016Z`, `cargo xtask sampling <target> --part mirror`,
2026-11-17: 261 to 335 `rows_births` in each of the six 10 s windows that began and
ended with births waiting and ran 958 to 1,131 frames, 96 to 113 frames a second; in
those windows 19 to 29 µs a birth, 2 to 4 births a pump, the call 40 to 48 µs). Three
more windows ended with births waiting and are left out of the range: one in which the
births began to arrive resolved 228, one in which a spawn's 1,212 births arrived resolved
none, and one that a stall cut to 427 frames resolved 49. The server test of S20 spawned
one group every 2 frames, about 55 a second.

**The loadout marks do not move the lag.** No mark is handed out while a birth waits, and
the model's lags are the same with a mark for every birth and with none.

## What changed

- **The spawn's handler binds** (quorum ruling of 2026-11-17). On `APPLIED` the
  `GROUP_SPAWN` handler reads each unit of the new group that exists, with the births
  tier's guarded read, and the agent writes their `OBJECT` records in the spawn's frame,
  before the `RESULT`, and on a replacing spawn before the old units' `OBJECT_GONE`
  (spec/commands.md, "Layout: GROUP_SPAWN"). The reads are in the command's `cost_us`
  and count as commanded work. A unit the handler cannot read is left to its birth or
  the next enumeration. The later birth of a unit the handler added costs the births
  tier one `wants` check. `live` keeps its meaning; spec/world.md, "Bound units", says
  which units are bound when a group turns `live` and what a call on an unbound unit
  does. `STATIC_SPAWN` is unchanged: a static binds when its birth resolves.
- **Births resolve oldest first** (`lua/agent/shim.lua`, a queue), for every object the
  births tier still reads: a mission's own units, players' aircraft, statics, and a unit
  the handler could not read. A row trails its birth by the births ahead of it and never
  by those born after it (spec/sampling.md, "Tiers").
- **A unit no player occupies gets no loadout mark** (below).
- **A test diagnostic for the lag.** With `NANOPROBE_COMMIT_TIMINGS` set, the sidecar
  times each unit from its spawn's applied result to the `OBJECT` record that binds it
  and prints a `bind_timings` line with each timing report, with the count of units
  whose `OBJECT` came after the result. The server test reads the lines and reports the
  lag over the run (`bind_lag` in its results).

An effect on a unit that is not bound is refused (spec/world.md, "Bound units"). The
server test counts the refusals (`unbound_hit_tries`).

Host tests: the bind model (`a_spawned_unit_has_its_row_in_the_spawns_frame`: no row
after the spawn's frame and one `OBJECT` for each unit; the agent's own share is 0.154 %
against 0.163 % with the births tier reading the same units). The model's clock holds
the agent's own time and leaves the handler's reads out, as commanded work, so the
comparison shows that the births the tier still receives, each a pump's call and a
`wants` check, cost no more than the reads they replace. It does not measure the
handler's reads; `lua/tests/spawn_bind.lua` shows they are in the command's `cost_us`.
The other host tests: the reconciler on the fake sim
(`every_unit_of_a_group_is_bound_when_the_group_turns_live`;
`a_new_object_ahead_of_the_old_object_s_gone_keeps_the_unit_bound`, for `DESTROYED`,
`DEAD`, `MISSING` and `REMOVED`); `lua/tests/births.lua` (the order, the queue's bound of
4,096 and its restart when it empties); and `lua/tests/spawn_bind.lua` with the real shim
and agent on the mock: N `OBJECT` records before the `RESULT` in one frame, one `OBJECT`
for each unit however many frames follow, each unit's later birth answered by a `wants`
check that takes no row, the replace's order (the old units' IDs handed to the call that
writes their `OBJECT_GONE`, after the new `OBJECT`s), a unit whose read raises (still
`APPLIED`, bound at its birth), the reads in `cost_us`, and a spawn past `commanded_ms`
counted in `commands_over_budget`. That Lua test reads the order from the agent's own
calls, not from the ring's bytes.

### The births' order and the rehearsal at a tenth of the budget

`lua/tests/sampling.lua` in its `tenth` mode has `link-probe` subscribe the first 20
unit rows of its mirror, and checks that at a tenth of the sampling budget the subscribed
rows keep at least 0.9 of their rate. It counts the subscribed rows sampled in each 10 s
report, so a subscribed unit that dies reads as a rate that fell. The mirror's first rows
are the units whose rows the agent added first: with births resolved oldest first, the
first units born. The test also kills 10 units at the tenth, the last unit of each of its
first ground groups, and 2 of those were among the 20 subscribed.

Measured on the development machine, 2026-11-18, the same test on two builds run one
after the other under the same load: the agent and shim of main `a7ee5af` (births newest
first) and this step's (`lua/tests/sampling.lua <module with fault injection> ... tenth`,
on the mock):

The test's deaths leave the subscribed units alone: it kills the last unit of the first
10 ground groups whose last unit is not subscribed, and checks that 10 died. Its bound is
0.9, as on main, and it has the same checks. The agent's rate for a subscribed row is the
same on both builds, 20 rows a second for 20 live units.

## Loadout marks

Every unit birth and takeoff left a mark, AI units included, and the pump asked each
marked unit for its player name. On the server a mark cost about 22 µs and was priced at
a vitals row, about 1 µs (LIVE, docs/results/2026-11-17-s19-spawn-state.md). In that
run's reports the marks left over from a spawn of 1,200 or 2,000 drained at about 11 a
second, about one a pump with 100 to 120 pumps in a 10 s window, and the vitals cursor
read 0 to 70 rows of the 200 to 330 due in each window until the list was empty. The
host model does not show that starvation: with a mark for each of 1,000 births it puts 1
to 3 frames over 250 µs and keeps the vitals rate.

The agent marks only a unit a player occupies:

- A birth is marked when a connected player holds the slot the unit was born in: the
  birth event's mission ID equals the player's slot ID. The agent keeps each connection's
  slot from the `PLAYER` records it already writes.
- A takeoff is marked when the unit's birth was marked.

An AI unit's birth or takeoff costs the pump nothing. The shim still asks a marked unit
for its player name before it reads the ammunition.

Evidence for the join (LIVE, the recording of the loadout check of
docs/results/2026-11-17-s19-spawn-state.md, `link-probe --record` attached to agent
`ff9c364bba4372c6` while a client took a slot and entered it; the recording is server
data and stays out of the repository; DCS 2.9.31.29434 under Wine 10.0, 2026-11-17): the `PLAYER` record of the slot change carried slot `8580`, 115.9 s into the
recording; the `S_EVENT_BIRTH` of the client's A-10C_2 carried mission ID `8580`, at
163.1 s. An AI M-113 spawned by script in the same recording carried mission ID
`1000005`. The player held the slot 47 s before the birth.

Not covered:

- A dynamic-spawn slot: whether its slot ID equals its unit's mission ID is UNVERIFIED.
  Where it does not, the unit's birth and takeoff are not read. The sidecar joins a
  player to a unit by the same rule (spec/world.md, "Players"), so the loadout would have
  no player to go to there either.
- A player's birth and takeoff reads were not run live in this step: no client was
  available. `lua/tests/loadout.lua` checks them on the mock, with the player holding the
  slot before the birth.
- A mark still costs more than the vitals row it is priced at: about 22 µs for the
  `getPlayerName` call, measured on AI units; the `getAmmo` call of a player's unit adds a
  cost that is UNVERIFIED. Marks come only for players' units, a few at a time.
- A birth that comes before its slot's `PLAYER` record: the unit's birth and takeoff are
  not read. The one live observation had the slot 47 s before the birth.
- The player who hosts a server from the client: the server's own connection writes no
  `PLAYER` record, so it holds no slot and its aircraft has no loadout read.
- A mission load: the agent does not clear the held slots then. Whether DCS reports a
  slot change for each connected player on a load is UNVERIFIED; a slot left held costs
  one `getPlayerName` call for a unit of the next mission whose mission ID equals it.

## What the fuel and ammunition removal bought

The owner asked what removing the unit fuel reads, the ammunition tier and AI ammunition
bought. The A/B of docs/results/2026-11-17-s19-spawn-state.md mixed the removal with the
AI loadout marks, which cost more than the removal saved. With the marks gone, the same
test at 2,000 objects, three builds:

| Measure | Before the removal (`6f50c62`) | Main with the marks (S19's branch run) | This build (run 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean share from the 800 spawn | 0.225 % | 0.236 % | 0.225 % |
| Mean share at 2,000, settled | 0.209 % | 0.229 % | 0.216 % |
| Largest 10 s window | 0.296 % | 0.307 % | 0.298 % |
| Frames over 250 µs | 112 of 41,896 (0.27 %) | 92 of 42,694 (0.22 %) | 99 of 41,555 (0.24 %) |
| Median position interval, stall-free | 1.006 s | 1.001 s | 1.003 s |
| Worst stall-free window | 3.66 s | 1.08 s | 1.08 s |
| Fill-window position | 1.08 s | 0.98 s | 1.06 s |
| Fill time, 800 births | 37 s | 35 s | 33 s |
| Vitals as a rate at 2,000, settled | 4.13 s | 40.2 s | 4.63 s |
| Pumps a second at 2,000, settled | 6.95 | 7.84 | 7.32 |
| DCS frames a second at 2,000 (5th percentile) | 52.2 (0.39) | 65.9 (0.67) | 53.1 (0.41) |

The first two columns are from that document; all three runs are on the same server and
DCS build, on two days.

What the removal bought, measured: nothing that these runs can tell from their own
variance. The mean share is 0.225 % before the removal and 0.225 % after it with the
marks fixed, and settled it is 0.209 % against 0.216 %. Positions keep their 1 s period
in all three. Vitals are at their rate before the removal and after it (4.13 s and
4.63 s against a 5 s target); the 40.2 s in between was the marks. Pumps a second and
frames over 250 µs move by less than they move between two runs of one sampler: the
same test on the person-fixes step's agent `9813a3d3bf40fc71`, whose sampler is main's
(bundle `sampling-<target>-20261118T002330Z`, 2026-11-17), read a mean share of 0.274 %
from the 800 spawn, against the 0.236 % of the middle column. Frame rate and stalls vary
from run to run on this server.

The removal's gain is in what the platform does not carry: no ammunition tier and its
rate, no fuel and ammunition fields in the specs, the store and the API, and no restore
of either. On agent time at 2,000 objects it is inside the noise, as the budget is a
share the sampler spends on whatever tiers remain.
