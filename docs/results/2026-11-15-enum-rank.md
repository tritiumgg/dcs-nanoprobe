## The test

1,960 moving units and 40 statics, `overhead_share` 0.003, scale 1. Costs (PRIOR, S10 and
S11): `coalition.getGroups` steps of 687 and 1,140 µs in turn, one per 100 enumeration
items; a birth 37 µs; the cross-state call 23 µs; a pump batch's fixed part 20 µs; a
position row 0.21 µs; the sweep's fixed part 15 µs; a system-call frame 165 µs a second.
Every unit moves, so every row is read on every position sweep. Five units are born every
5 s, some while an enumeration runs. The measure starts 10 s after the fill's 2,000 births
resolve and lasts 180 s, three enumerations.

The pass, from the ruling: in every 10 s window the largest per-row positions gap is at
most 1.25 s and the median at most 1.05 s; each enumeration ends within its period plus its
duration of the last one's `ENUM_BEGIN`; every birth resolves within one enumeration
period plus the longest enumeration's duration (3a); the mean share is within
`overhead_share`; the cap forgives no debt. The test checks the enumeration's end to 1 s,
the length of its steps.

`Sampler::sweep_wait` records on each frame why the sweep read no row when it owed some:
the subscribed pump, the minimum batch, the debt's repayment, the savings, or the slice
with no repayment to blame. The `sampling` LOG carries the counts per window
(`sweep_wait_subscribed`, `sweep_wait_batch`, `sweep_wait_debt`, `sweep_wait_fund`,
`sweep_wait_slice`) and the balance (`maintenance_us`).

The "Main" column is the same test over main's sampler: `crates/agent-link/src/sampler.rs`
of commit `0ffcff8` (main `e114f8e` with only the `SweepWait` record added), under this
tree's `sampler_tests.rs` less the tests that name what main lacks
(`every_sweep_wait_is_recorded_and_counted`, `births_go_before_a_running_enumeration`,
`the_balance_pays_its_own_time_past_the_floor_and_down_to_its_bound`). Main's record has
four reasons: its debt count holds every frame on which the savings paid a row and the
slice did not, whether or not a repayment cut the slice. This tree splits those into debt
and slice.

On main the largest gaps fall in the enumeration windows, and births wait behind the
running enumeration. At 110 frames a second the sweep owed rows and read none on 11,326
frames in 180 s for its minimum batch, 2,359 for a short slice (main's debt count) and
1,376 for its savings. Each dear step left
700-1,100 µs of debt. Its repayment, `A` / 2 a frame, cut the slice under the sweep's
64-row minimum, so the sweep held for 0.4-0.5 s after each step. The debt cleared in under
a second and the sweep read between steps. Positions therefore slowed during an
enumeration but did not stop.

On this tree the debt stays near 0 through an enumeration: the balance pays the steps.
At 110 frames a second the sweep still waits for its minimum batch on 12,502 frames in
180 s, about 70 a second: the cadence of a 64-row sweep on about half a frame's allowance.
It waits for its savings on 2,134 frames and for debt on 43; for the subscribed pump and
for a short slice with no repayment to blame on none. That debt is the row tiers' own: a
pump that reads rows as well as births or the enumeration charges its call and closes to
the row tiers. A reviewer's instrumented copy, at an earlier 60 such frames, put them all
inside enumeration spans with a debt of 9-18 µs (UNVERIFIED: no script was kept).
Charging the balance a share of a mixed pump's call and closes by batch time cut the
count from 60 to 44, but held the enumeration longer, and vitals then missed its band in
two existing tests (UNVERIFIED: the trial was reverted and is not committed). The share
stays with the rows, and the test holds these waits under 1 % of frames.

## What changed

- Births and the enumeration keep their own account (spec/sampling.md, "Slice" and
  "Tiers"). The time their balance pays leaves the row tiers' debt, and what the balance
  earns leaves the row tiers' allowance. At scale 1 a pump that only resolved births or
  read the enumeration, and made no existence checks, pays its call and closes from the
  balance too, less any commanded work inside the call. No maintenance time reaches the
  row tiers' debt, as ruling item 2 says ("never"). The −1,000 µs floor still stops the
  scale's surcharge. Their own time may take the balance past it, down to the debt's
  bound of one second's budget (−3,000 µs at 0.3 %); a step of 1,140 µs taken at a
  balance of 69 µs leaves it at −1,071 µs. Past the bound, as in a stall inside a step,
  the time is forgiven and counted in `maintenance_forgiven_us`. The coordinator was
  asked to confirm this reading of item 2, whose floor and "never" conflict for a step
  that passes the floor. The balance earns while births or an enumeration wait, and
  otherwise only back to 0.
- The sweep saves the row tiers' allowance at scale 1. It still takes at most 1 − `m` of a
  frame while a tier above it waits for a pump, so a pump has room in each frame. Below
  scale 1 its savings follow the tenth's rules as before.
- Births go before a running enumeration. The `wants` drop and the 3a bound stay.
- A pump whose batches were all births or the enumeration does not decay the estimate of a
  row batch's fixed part, which only row batches teach.

`m` = 0.5 / 0.75 and the 3b nonzero subscribed rate are unchanged. The tenth tests pass
unchanged. New unit tests drive each wait reason, the births-first order and the
balance's bound; each fails when its rule is reverted.

## Tests changed, and why

- `the_live_cost_storm_case_holds_every_tier_at_k_1`: the start runs 240 s, not 60 s. With
  births first, the fill's 2,000 births at 37 µs each resolve before the first enumeration
  reads on, so that enumeration lasts about 106 s and the reported period holds it until
  two more have run. The simulated shim reads a new object in an enumeration at 1 µs and a
  birth at 37 µs, so the fill was cheap through the enumeration and is not through births.
- `every_tier_holds`: vitals is judged on the mean period of the last minute in every case,
  as the attitude case already was. One reading scatters from 4.2 to 5.4 s, because a
  vitals sweep that repays rows after a hold reads under its period; the means are
  4.99-5.07 s.
- `cycle_capped` (the vitals-repay tests): the subscribed baseline is taken after the
  fill's births resolve, since births first can make the fill outlast the first 90 s.

## Open

- The live hold of 6-27 s with no position row (S11 run 2, rerun 19) is not explained. The
  host shows only a 0.4-0.5 s hold per step on main. A whole-enumeration hold needs the
  balance to recover faster than the debt clears (`m` = 0.75, an enumeration overdue), or a
  cost the host does not model: DCS stall frames, or fewer and larger steps (UNVERIFIED). A
  live run on this tree traces it with the new `sampling` keys.
- 4 s after the fill's last birth, at 110 frames a second, one gap reads 1.26 s. The test
  starts its measure 10 s after the fill, whose window S11 judges apart.
- The agent's frame time and share against the S11 baseline: the change adds per-frame
  accounting inside the agent, and CLAUDE.md asks for a before-and-after measure. It runs
  on the coordinator-approved live run, beside the trace of the hold.
- Births before the enumeration make a fill from empty resolve at a birth's cost; in the
  simulated shim the enumeration filled it at 1 µs an object. A live fill measures the
  real difference.

## S14 lead

S14's mirror windows of 3.07-3.99 s do not overlap an enumeration or a spawn fill (LOG,
read from four stored mirror bundles: `sampling-<target>-20261114T211050Z` (S14 run 1,
3.46 s), `...T231259Z` (run 2, 3.07 s), `...T234008Z` (A/B main, 3.99 s) and
`...T234940Z` (A/B final tree, 1.08 s); DCS 2.9.31.29434, Wine 10.0, 2026-11-14, PRIOR from
the S14 results). They were read by hand: the `sampling` reports in `mirror-probe.jsonl`
(`rows_positions`, `rows_enumeration`, `rows_births`, `debt_us`, `gap_max_s` per window) and
the `ENUM_BEGIN`/`ENUM_END` records in `mirror-recording.jsonl`. No script was written for
it. Each high window has no enumeration rows
and no births, and its debt is 0. Each one ends with a frame gap of 5.7-5.9 s, which lands
at the window's report while the window's own `gap_max_s` reads 0.02 s. The window is
therefore counted stall-free. The next window shows the gap, about 3 s. Clean windows,
including ones inside an enumeration, read 0.98-1.04 s. The enumeration's rank therefore
does not explain them; a DCS stall at a window's edge, counted in the wrong window, fits
(UNVERIFIED). The measure's window boundary is an open item for the tool that judges it
(`crates/xtask/src/sampling_live.rs`).
