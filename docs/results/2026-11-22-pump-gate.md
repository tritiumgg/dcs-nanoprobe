## The rule

All in `Sampler::gate` and `Sampler::sweep` (`crates/agent-link/src/sampler.rs`).

- `reach` counts credit only up to the slice's cap, so three calls and fixed parts above
  what a frame can have no longer keep the gate shut while the call's estimate never decays.
- The floor `N` is the measured call `x̄`, which does not decay between pumps, the larger
  fixed part of the row tiers and of births and the enumeration, and the close. The decayed
  estimate `x` can no longer open a pump on a frame that cannot pay one real call.
- Where `N` passes 0.8 of the most a frame can have (a slow host), the floor stops there,
  and the pump it lets through is counted (`clamped_pumps`).
- After the gate shuts for room while a pump waits, the next sweep holds min(max(`N` / 0.8,
  `N` + `r_s`), `T`) of the frame for the pump, its savings included. A waiting pump never
  waits for good.
- `r_s`, the subscribed row: a pump for the subscribed rows read with their attitude forces
  its first row even when it does not fit. Where the most a frame can have comes within that
  row of the cap, the floor holds it. Whether that tier plans the pump's first batch is the
  planner's own test (`Sampler::maintenance_rows`): births or the enumeration go first only
  when they would take a batch on the budget the pump would have.
- Estimate corrections: a first batch whose one step is dear keeps the tier's fixed part;
  a row batch without two clock reads learns its fixed part from its time less its rows;
  the fixed part decays only on a pump that planned a row batch and did not measure it;
  births and the enumeration stop a batch once what is left no longer pays their planned
  steps; the sweep's forced row waits, after a shut for room, until the sweep is late.

## Host survey

`survey_the_gate`, proptest seed 20261120, 4,000 cases of 50 s: share 0.001 to 0.3, 20-250
frames a second, 30-2,000 units, the call 5-62.5 µs and a batch's fixed part 0-62.5 µs,
births at 20 s, urgent checks from 20 to 45 s, enumerations, twenty attitude rows. Each
case counts the failure kinds of `wait_failures`. Both columns ran in the same harness.

| Kind | This rule | Release 0.0 | This rule only, share ≥ 0.003 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frames over 250 µs past 0.1 % of the case's frames | 0 | 172 | 0 |
| Births not cleared within 120 s at the last 10 s' rate | 325 | 1,861 | 0 |
| An urgent check still waits at 50 s | 30 | 979 | 0 |
| 5 s with no pump while work waits | 218 | 2,164 | 0 |
| 5 s with no sweep | 12 | 86 | 0 |
| 5 s with no sweep, waiting mostly for the attitude rows' pump | 37 | 103 | 18 |

Every failure of this rule but the last row is at share 0.001, where the budget starves
births and checks under both rules. The last row is the attitude-row hold: the sweep waits
for a pump of the subscribed attitude rows that the gate holds back (more than half of the
window's waiting frames are `SweepWait::Subscribed`). It is an open item shared with
release 0.0.

Uncounted frames over 250 µs, outside the first pump, the clamp, an unmeasured tier's
first batch and a dear enumeration step: 23 in 26.8 million frames under this rule, 26,442
under release 0.0. Each was traced:

| Class | Frames | Over 250 µs by |
|---|---|---|
| A lone row: a first batch whose one row does not fit, planned at its exact cost (counted in `lone_rows`) | 14 | 0.5-25.3 µs |
| An enumeration batch at its mean step cost whose dearer step every hundred items fell after the last clock read | 9 | 0.1-1.6 µs |

None comes from a biased or stuck estimate.

The forced subscribed row acts where the cap binds: it decided 304 gates in a case at share
0.05 and 191 frames a second, which without it ran 299 of 9,500 frames 2 to 6 µs past
250 µs. In host normal play at the default share it decides no gate, at 30 or 40 frames a
second either, so normal play there is that of release 0.0
(`at_30_frames_a_second_the_default_share_holds_the_row_as_main_plays`,
`normal_play_holds_the_owners_targets_and_reads_every_tier_as_main_does`).

A further share-0.003 run stopped at the 1,200-object dump, before any judge: the
probe's dump held no `periods`, and the tool stops on that. DCS and the agent ran on
(LIVE). The probe reads the status metrics once under their sequence lock, with no retry
(`crates/fake-agent/src/bin/link-probe.rs`), so a read that overlaps the agent's write
returns nothing; that this is what happened is UNVERIFIED (read from the code).

The final build fails one ruled bound in every share-0.003 run, the pump bound (pumps a
second within the bound at k = 1), as release 0.0 does; release 0.0 also fails the vitals
band and forgives debt after the 800-object spawn. The first final run's 1,200-object
compare found moving rows read every 0.584 s against a reported period of 0.943 s, outside
its 25 % band: the sweep was catching up on positions owed after the fill (117 and 104
sweeps in a window against 92-95). Reading faster than the period is not a stall, and the
next run read 0.964 s against 0.964 s.

## The stall-free window

The positions judge leaves out windows a DCS stall spoils. A window is a stall window when
the longest gap between two DCS frames ending in it passes 1 s, and the window after it is
left out too. The stall's start was not: a gap counts in the window where it ends, so the
window in which a stall began reads a position period that spans the stall while its own
`gap_max_s` is short. One run failed on such a window: 3.03 s, `gap_max_s` 0.02 s, before a
window of 5 frames in 10.98 s at an agent share of 0.00014 (LIVE). The judge
(`crates/xtask/src/sampling_positions.rs`, `classify`) now also leaves out every window
whose position period meets a stall's possible span, the stall window's span widened by
its gap before its start (host test
`the_windows_where_a_stall_begins_and_ends_are_both_stalled`). The stall threshold is
unchanged. All share-0.003 runs above are judged by it: release 0.0 and the first two
runs of this rule, judged again from their bundles, read 1.008, 1.069 and 3.034 s before
(the last a failure), and the third run was judged by it live.

## Open items

Shared with release 0.0, for the release notes:

- At share 0.001 the budget starves births and urgent checks under both rules.
- The attitude-row hold (above). On a slow host under the clamp, attitude rows alone get
  their pumps twice a second, and the sweep reads nothing while they run.
- A tier's first batch before its cost is measured plans on a guess and may pass 250 µs
  once a tier.
- The warehouse estimate settles at the cheapest airbase's read, so a dear airbase's read
  is planned short.
- The starvation guard's late row after births fill a pump.
- The gate decides on the state before the pump: credit the pump accrues as it begins, or
  a call slower than its estimate while in debt, can still leave the forced row to a lone
  row (1 frame at 251.4 µs in the survey).
- Test tooling: the probe reads the status metrics once, with no retry; one live run
  stopped on a dump without them (above).
- Slow serves at share 0.003 and 200-230 frames a second: a 1,443-unit roster resolves
  about 13 births a second.

## Limits

The survey is the host model: the simulated shim at fixed costs, one seed. The live runs
are one server, one mission and one DCS build, at two shares.
